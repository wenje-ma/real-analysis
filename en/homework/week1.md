### Problem 1

Let $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. Prove **Proposition 4.1.2**: $\mathcal A$ is an algebra if and only if $\mathcal A$ satisfies

(a) $X\in\mathcal A$;

(b) closed under complementation: $A\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A^{c}\in\mathcal A$;

(c) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

### Solution to Problem 1

By **Definition 4.1.1 (Algebra of Sets)** and **Proposition 4.1.2**, we prove the two directions.

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal A$ is an algebra. Then $X\in\mathcal A$, so (a) holds, and closure under finite unions gives (c). For closure under complementation (b): for any $A\in\mathcal A$, from $X,A\in\mathcal A$ and closure under set difference, we get

$$
A^{c}=X\setminus A\in\mathcal A.
$$

Thus (a), (b) and (c) all hold.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose (a), (b) and (c) hold. $X\in\mathcal A$ is exactly Definition 4.1.1 (1); closure under finite unions is (3). It remains to verify closure under set difference (2): for any $A,B\in\mathcal A$, (b) gives $B^{c}\in\mathcal A$, (c) gives $A^{c}\cup B\in\mathcal A$, and (b) again gives

$$
A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}=\left(A^{c}\cup B\right)^{c}\in\mathcal A,
$$

where the second equality uses **Theorem 1.2.9 (De Morgan's Laws)**. Hence $\mathcal A$ is an algebra.

Combining the two directions, the proposition is proved. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 2

### Solution to Problem 2

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal F$ is a $\sigma$-algebra (**Definition 4.1.3**). Then $X\in\mathcal F$; it is closed under complementation (b); it is closed under countable unions, in particular under finite unions (c), and also under countable unions of pairwise disjoint sets. It remains to verify closure under set difference: for $A,B\in\mathcal F$, by (b), (c) and De Morgan's laws

$$
A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}=\left(A^{c}\cup B\right)^{c}\in\mathcal F.
$$

Hence by **Proposition 4.1.2**, $\mathcal F$ is an algebra, and it is clearly closed under countable unions of pairwise disjoint sets.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal F$ is an algebra and is closed under countable unions of pairwise disjoint sets. Being an algebra gives $X\in\mathcal F$, closure under complementation and closure under finite unions; it remains to prove closure under countable unions. Let $\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ and **disjointify** them:

$$
B_{1}:=A_{1},;B_{n}:=A_{n}\setminus\left(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n-1}\right)\left(n\ge2\right).

$$
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal F.

Therefore $\mathcal F$ is closed under countable unions and is a $\sigma$-algebra. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 5

### Solution to Problem 5

Write $\tau$= the family of open sets, $\mathcal F$= the family of closed sets, $\mathcal G=\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}$, $\mathcal H=\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}$, $\mathcal K=\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}$. By **Definition 4.3.2**, $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.

**1. $\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.**

Closed sets are complements of open sets, and open sets are complements of closed sets. For any closed set $C$, $C^{c}$ is open, so $C=\left(C^{c}\right)^{c}\in\sigma\left(\tau\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under complementation); hence $\mathcal F\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Symmetrically, for any open set $O$, $O^{c}$ is closed, so $O=\left(O^{c}\right)^{c}\in\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)$; hence $\tau\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)$. The two are equal.

**2. $\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.**

Every open interval $\left(a,b\right)$ is an open set, so $\mathcal G\subset\tau\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Conversely, by **Theorem 3.2.1**, every open set $O$ in $\mathbb R$ is a union of countably many pairwise disjoint open intervals: $O=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_{n},b_{n}\right)\in\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under countable unions); hence $\tau\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)$. The two are equal.

**3. $\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.**

Every closed interval $\left[a,b\right]$ is a closed set, so $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal F\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Conversely, open intervals can be approximated from below by closed intervals:

$$
\left(a,b\right)=\bigcup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\left[a+\frac{1}{n},b-\frac{1}{n}\right]\in\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)
$$

(take $n_{0}$ such that $a+\frac1n< b-\frac1n$); hence $\mathcal G\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)$. The two are equal.

**4. $\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.**

On one hand, $\left(a,b\right]=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a,b+\frac1n\right)$ is a countable intersection of open intervals, so $\left(a,b\right]\in\sigma\left(\tau\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under countable intersections: taking complements reduces to countable unions); hence $\mathcal K\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.

On the other hand, open intervals are approximated from below by left-open right-closed intervals: take $n_{0}$ such that $b-\frac1n> a$,

$$
\left(a,b\right)=\bigcup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\left(a,b-\frac1n\right]\in\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right),
$$

hence $\mathcal G\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)$. The two are equal.

In summary, the $\sigma$-algebras generated by the five families all equal $\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)$, and the proposition is proved. $\blacksquare$

### Problem 7

Prove **Theorem 4.5.4 (Products of Borel Sets)**: for the Borel $\sigma$-algebras on Euclidean spaces,

$$

### Solution to Problem 7

Let $\pi_{1}:\mathbb R^{m+n}\to\mathbb R^{m}$ and $\pi_{2}:\mathbb R^{m+n}\to\mathbb R^{n}$ be the two coordinate projections. We first establish an auxiliary fact: the projection $\pi_{i}$ is continuous, hence pulls Borel sets back to Borel sets.

The auxiliary fact holds.

**($\subset$) $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$.**

Take any measurable rectangle $A\times B$ ($A\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$, $B\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$). Then

$$
A\times B=\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(A\right)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}\left(B\right).
$$

By the auxiliary fact, $\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(A\right),\pi_{2}^{-1}\left(B\right)\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$, and their intersection is also in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$. Hence all measurable rectangles lie in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$; since $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing the measurable rectangles, the inclusion $\subseteq$ follows.

**($\supset$) $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$.**

By **Basic Knowledge**, every open set $O$ in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ can be written as a union of countably many open rectangles: $O=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(U_{k}\times V_{k}\right)$, where $U_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{m}$ and $V_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{n}$ are open. Each open rectangle $U_{k}\times V_{k}$ is a measurable rectangle ($U_{k}\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$, $V_{k}\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$), hence $\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$. Thus $O$, being a countable union, also belongs to this product $\sigma$-algebra. Therefore all open sets lie in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$, and hence

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)=\sigma\left(\text{开集}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right).
$$

Combining the two directions, $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

Thus all the generators $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(E_{\alpha}\right)$ ($\alpha\in I$) lie in the product $\sigma$-algebra, and since $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing these sets, the $\sigma$-algebra they generate satisfies

$$
\mathscr G\subseteq\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}.
$$

Combining the two directions,

$$
\boxed{\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}=\sigma\left(\left\{\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right):\alpha\in I\right\}\right)}\ \blacksquare
$$
