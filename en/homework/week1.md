# week1

> **Basic Knowledge: Open Sets as Countable Unions of Open Rectangles**<br>Every open set in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ can be written as a countable union of open rectangles: $O=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(U_{k}\times V_{k}\right)$, where $U_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{m}$, $V_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{n}$ are open sets (rectangles with rational endpoints form a countable basis).

> **Theorem 1.2.9 (De Morgan's Laws)**<br>$\left(A\cup B\right)^{c}=A^{c}\cap B^{c}$, $\left(A\cap B\right)^{c}=A^{c}\cup B^{c}$.

> **Theorem 2.4.2 (Properties of Preimage Operations)**<br>Let $f:A\to B$. Then $f^{-1}\left(B_{1}\cup B_{2}\right)=f^{-1}\left(B_{1}\right)\cup f^{-1}\left(B_{2}\right)$, $f^{-1}\left(B_{1}\cap B_{2}\right)=f^{-1}\left(B_{1}\right)\cap f^{-1}\left(B_{2}\right)$, $f^{-1}\left(B^{c}\right)=\left(f^{-1}\left(B\right)\right)^{c}$, $f^{-1}\left(\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}B_{\alpha}\right)=\bigcup_{\alpha\in I}f^{-1}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)$, $f^{-1}\left(\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}B_{\alpha}\right)=\bigcap_{\alpha\in I}f^{-1}\left(B_{\alpha}\right)$.

> **Theorem 3.2.1 (Structure of Open Sets in $\mathbb R$)**<br>If $U\subset\mathbb R$ is a nonempty open set, then there exists a sequence of pairwise disjoint open intervals $\left\{I_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}$ such that $U=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}I_{n}$; this representation is unique up to reordering.

> **Definition 4.1.1 (Algebra of Sets)**<br>Let $X$ be nonempty and $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. $\mathcal A$ is called an algebra of sets (Boolean algebra) on $X$ if: (1) $X\in\mathcal A$; (2) closed under differences: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\setminus B\in\mathcal A$; (3) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

> **Proposition 4.1.2 (Equivalent Definition of an Algebra)**<br>Let $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. The following are equivalent: (1) $\mathcal A$ is an algebra; (2) $\mathcal A$ satisfies: (a) $X\in\mathcal A$; (b) closed under complements: $A\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A^{c}=X\setminus A\in\mathcal A$; (c) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

> **Definition 4.1.3 ($\sigma$-Algebra)**<br>Let $X$ be nonempty and $\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. $\mathcal F$ is called a $\sigma$-algebra on $X$ if: (1) $X\in\mathcal F$; (2) closed under complements: $A\in\mathcal F\Rightarrow A^{c}\in\mathcal F$; (3) closed under countable unions: $\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F\Rightarrow\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal F$. $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ is called a measurable space, and elements of $\mathcal F$ are called measurable sets.

> **Definition 4.2.1 (Generated Algebra)**<br>The smallest algebra containing $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is called the algebra generated by $\mathcal E$, denoted $\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$:

$$
\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal A\text{ is an algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A\right\}.
$$

> **Definition 4.2.2 (Generated $\sigma$-Algebra)**<br>The smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is called the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal E$, denoted $\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$:

$$
\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal F\text{ is a }\sigma\text{-algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal F\right\}.
$$

> **Definition 4.3.2 (Borel $\sigma$-Algebra)**<br>Let $\left(X,\tau\right)$ be a topological space. The $\sigma$-algebra generated by all open sets is called the Borel $\sigma$-algebra, denoted $\mathcal B\left(X\right)$, and its elements are called Borel sets.

> **Example 4.3.3 (Borel Algebra on $\mathbb R$)**<br>On $\mathbb R$, the following families generate the same Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)$: all open sets $\tau$, all closed sets $\mathcal F$, all open intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}$, all closed intervals $\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}$, all half-open intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}$, i.e.

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}\right).
$$

> **Definition 4.5.1 (Measurable Rectangles and Product $\sigma$-Algebra)**<br>Let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal G\right)$ be measurable spaces. $A\times B\subseteq X\times Y$ is called a measurable rectangle if $A\in\mathcal F$ and $B\in\mathcal G$. The product $\sigma$-algebra is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing all measurable rectangles:

$$
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G:=\sigma\left(\left\{A\times B:A\in\mathcal F,B\in\mathcal G\right\}\right).
$$

> **Theorem 4.5.3 (Section Measurability)**<br>If $E\in\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$, then for every $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$, its sections are measurable:

$$
E_{x}=\left\{y\in Y:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal G,\qquad E^{y}=\left\{x\in X:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal F.
$$

> **Theorem 4.5.4 (Product of Borel $\sigma$-Algebras)**<br>For Borel $\sigma$-algebras in Euclidean space,

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right).
$$

> **Definition 4.5.7 (Arbitrary Product $\sigma$-Algebra)**<br>Let $\left\{\left(X_{\alpha},\mathcal F_{\alpha}\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in I}$ be a family of measurable spaces. The product $\sigma$-algebra $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by all cylinder sets

$$
\left\{x=\left(x_{\alpha}\right)_{\alpha\in I}\in\prod_{\alpha\in I}X_{\alpha}:x_{\beta}\in A_{\beta}\right\},\ A_{\beta}\in\mathcal F_{\beta},\ \beta\in I.
$$

## 1

Let $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. Prove **Proposition 4.1.2 (Equivalent Definition of an Algebra)**: $\mathcal A$ is an algebra iff $\mathcal A$ satisfies

(a) $X\in\mathcal A$;

(b) closed under complements: $A\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A^{c}\in\mathcal A$;

(c) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

### Solution 1

By **Definition 4.1.1 (Algebra of Sets)** and **Proposition 4.1.2 (Equivalent Definition of an Algebra)**, prove in two directions.

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal A$ is an algebra. Then $X\in\mathcal A$ so (a) holds, and finite unions are closed so (c) holds. Closure under complements (b): for any $A\in\mathcal A$, since $X,A\in\mathcal A$ and differences are closed,

$$
A^{c}=X\setminus A\in\mathcal A.
$$

Thus (a)(b)(c) all hold.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose (a)(b)(c) hold. $X\in\mathcal A$ is (1) of **Definition 4.1.1 (Algebra of Sets)**; finite union closure is (3). It remains to verify difference closure (2): for any $A,B\in\mathcal A$, (b) gives $B^{c}\in\mathcal A$, (c) gives $A^{c}\cup B\in\mathcal A$, and (b) again gives

$$
A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}=\left(A^{c}\cup B\right)^{c}\in\mathcal A,
$$

where the second equality uses **Theorem 1.2.9 (De Morgan's Laws)**. Thus $\mathcal A$ is an algebra.

Combining both directions proves the proposition. $\blacksquare$

## 2

Prove: $\mathcal F$ is a $\sigma$-algebra iff $\mathcal F$ is an algebra and is closed under countable disjoint unions.

### Solution 2

**($\Rightarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal F$ is a $\sigma$-algebra (**Definition 4.1.3 ($\sigma$-Algebra)**). Then $X\in\mathcal F$; complements are closed (b); countable unions are closed, hence in particular finite unions (c), and countable disjoint unions are closed. It remains to verify difference closure: for $A,B\in\mathcal F$, by (b)(c) and De Morgan's laws,

$$
A\setminus B=A\cap B^{c}=\left(A^{c}\cup B\right)^{c}\in\mathcal F.
$$

Thus by **Proposition 4.1.2 (Equivalent Definition of an Algebra)**, $\mathcal F$ is an algebra, and it is clearly closed under countable disjoint unions.

**($\Leftarrow$)** Suppose $\mathcal F$ is an algebra and is closed under countable disjoint unions. The algebra gives $X\in\mathcal F$, complement closure, and finite union closure; it remains to prove countable union closure. Let $\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ and disjointify:

$$
B_{1}:=A_{1},\qquad B_{n}:=A_{n}\setminus\left(A_{1}\cup\cdots\cup A_{n-1}\right)\ (n\ge2).
$$

By difference and finite-union closure of the algebra, each $B_{n}\in\mathcal F$; and $\left\{B_{n}\right\}$ are pairwise disjoint while preserving the union: $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}$. By countable disjoint union closure,

$$
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}B_{n}\in\mathcal F.
$$

Thus $\mathcal F$ is closed under countable unions and is a $\sigma$-algebra. $\blacksquare$

## 3

Let $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. Prove that the algebra generated by $\mathcal E$,

$$
\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal A\text{ is an algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A\right\},
$$

is an algebra.

### Solution 3

Let $\mathscr C=\left\{\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal A\text{ is an algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A\right\}$ be the family of all algebras containing $\mathcal E$. $\mathscr C$ is nonempty (since $\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is an algebra containing $\mathcal E$), so by **Definition 4.2.1 (Generated Algebra)**, $\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$ is well-defined.

Verify **Definition 4.1.1 (Algebra of Sets)** item by item for $\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$:

(1) **$X\in\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$**: each $\mathcal A\in\mathscr C$ is an algebra, so $X\in\mathcal A$, hence $X$ belongs to the intersection of all $\mathcal A$, i.e. $X\in\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$.

(2) **Difference closure**: let $A,B\in\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$. Then for each $\mathcal A\in\mathscr C$, $A,B\in\mathcal A$, and since $\mathcal A$ is an algebra, $A\setminus B\in\mathcal A$. Since $\mathcal A$ is arbitrary, $A\setminus B\in\bigcap\mathscr C=\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$.

(3) **Finite union closure**: similarly, for each $\mathcal A\in\mathscr C$, $A\cup B\in\mathcal A$, so $A\cup B\in\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$.

Thus $\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$ is an algebra containing $\mathcal E$. By definition it is the intersection of all algebras containing $\mathcal E$, so it is the smallest algebra containing $\mathcal E$. $\blacksquare$

## 4

Let $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. Prove that the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal E$,

$$
\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal F\text{ is a }\sigma\text{-algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal F\right\},
$$

is a $\sigma$-algebra.

### Solution 4

Let $\mathscr C'=\left\{\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal F\text{ is a }\sigma\text{-algebra and }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal F\right\}$. $\mathscr C'$ is nonempty ($\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal E$), so by **Definition 4.2.2 (Generated $\sigma$-Algebra)**, $\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$ is well-defined.

Verify **Definition 4.1.3 ($\sigma$-Algebra)** item by item:

(1) **$X\in\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$**: each $\mathcal F\in\mathscr C'$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $X$, so $X$ belongs to their intersection.

(2) **Complement closure**: let $A\in\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$. Then for each $\mathcal F\in\mathscr C'$, $A\in\mathcal F$, and since $\mathcal F$ is a $\sigma$-algebra, $A^{c}\in\mathcal F$, hence $A^{c}\in\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$.

(3) **Countable union closure**: let $\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$. Then for each $\mathcal F\in\mathscr C'$, $\left\{A_{n}\right\}\subset\mathcal F$, and since $\mathcal F$ is closed under countable unions, $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal F$, hence $\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$.

Thus $\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal E$, and it is the smallest one (the intersection of all $\sigma$-algebras containing $\mathcal E$). $\blacksquare$

## 5

On $\mathbb R$, prove **Example 4.3.3 (Borel Algebra on $\mathbb R$)**: the following families generate the same Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)$:

all open sets $\tau$, all closed sets $\mathcal F$, all open intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}$, all closed intervals $\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}$, all half-open intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}$.

That is, prove

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}\right)=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}\right).
$$

### Solution 5

Let $\tau$ be the open sets, $\mathcal F$ the closed sets, $\mathcal G=\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}$, $\mathcal H=\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}$, $\mathcal K=\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}$. By **Definition 4.3.2 (Borel $\sigma$-Algebra)**, $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.

**1. $\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.** Closed sets are complements of open sets and vice versa. For any closed set $C$, $C^{c}$ is open, so $C=\left(C^{c}\right)^{c}\in\sigma\left(\tau\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under complements), hence $\mathcal F\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Symmetrically, for any open set $O$, $O^{c}$ is closed, $O=\left(O^{c}\right)^{c}\in\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)$, so $\tau\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)$. They are equal.

**2. $\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.** Every open interval $\left(a,b\right)$ is open, so $\mathcal G\subset\tau\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Conversely, by **Theorem 3.2.1 (Structure of Open Sets in $\mathbb R$)**, every open set $O$ in $\mathbb R$ is a countable union of disjoint open intervals: $O=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a_{n},b_{n}\right)\in\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under countable unions), so $\tau\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)$. They are equal.

**3. $\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.** Every closed interval $\left[a,b\right]$ is closed, so $\mathcal H\subset\mathcal F\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$. Conversely, open intervals can be approximated from within by closed intervals:

$$
\left(a,b\right)=\bigcup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\left[a+\frac{1}{n},b-\frac{1}{n}\right]\in\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)
$$

(take $n_{0}$ so that $a+\frac1n< b-\frac1n$), hence $\mathcal G\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal H\right)$. They are equal.

**4. $\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)=\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.** On one hand, $\left(a,b\right]=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(a,b+\frac1n\right)$ is a countable intersection of open intervals, so $\left(a,b\right]\in\sigma\left(\tau\right)$ (a $\sigma$-algebra is closed under countable intersections: take complements to get countable unions), hence $\mathcal K\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)\subset\sigma\left(\tau\right)$.

On the other hand, open intervals are approximated from within by half-open intervals: take $n_{0}$ so that $b-\frac1n> a$,

$$
\left(a,b\right)=\bigcup_{n=n_{0}}^{\infty}\left(a,b-\frac1n\right]\in\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right),
$$

hence $\mathcal G\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)\Rightarrow\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\sigma\left(\mathcal G\right)\subset\sigma\left(\mathcal K\right)$. They are equal.

Thus all five families generate the same $\sigma$-algebra $\sigma\left(\tau\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)$, proving the proposition. $\blacksquare$

## 6

Prove **Theorem 4.5.3 (Section Measurability)**: let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal G\right)$ be measurable spaces. If $E\in\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$, then for every $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$, its sections

$$
E_{x}=\left\{y\in Y:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal G,\qquad E^{y}=\left\{x\in X:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal F.
$$

### Solution 6

Fix $x\in X$ and consider the family

$$
\mathscr C:=\left\{E\subseteq X\times Y:E_{x}\in\mathcal G\right\}.
$$

We prove that $\mathscr C$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing all measurable rectangles, hence $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G=\sigma\left(\text{measurable rectangles}\right)\subseteq\mathscr C$, i.e. every $E\in\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$ has $E_{x}\in\mathcal G$. The $y$-side is entirely symmetric.

**$\mathscr C$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.**

(1) **$X\times Y\in\mathscr C$**: $\left(X\times Y\right)_{x}=Y\in\mathcal G$.

(2) **Complement closure**: if $E\in\mathscr C$, i.e. $E_{x}\in\mathcal G$, then $\left(E^{c}\right)_{x}=\left(E_{x}\right)^{c}\in\mathcal G$ (since $\mathcal G$ is a $\sigma$-algebra closed under complements), so $E^{c}\in\mathscr C$.

(3) **Countable union closure**: if $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathscr C$, then $\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)_{x}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(E_{n}\right)_{x}\in\mathcal G$ ($\mathcal G$ is closed under countable unions), so $\bigcup E_{n}\in\mathscr C$.

By **Definition 4.1.3 ($\sigma$-Algebra)**, $\mathscr C$ is a $\sigma$-algebra.

**$\mathscr C$ contains all measurable rectangles.** Let $A\in\mathcal F$, $B\in\mathcal G$. Then

$$
\left(A\times B\right)_{x}=\begin{cases}B,&x\in A,\\\varnothing,&x\notin A.\end{cases}
$$

Both cases lie in $\mathcal G$, so $A\times B\in\mathscr C$.

Thus $\mathscr C$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing all measurable rectangles, and $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing them (**Definition 4.5.1 (Measurable Rectangles and Product $\sigma$-Algebra)**), so $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G\subseteq\mathscr C$. Hence for every $E\in\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$, $E_{x}\in\mathcal G$.

Symmetrically (fix $y\in Y$, take the section $E^{y}$, and argue identically with $\mathcal F$), we get $E^{y}\in\mathcal F$. $\blacksquare$

## 7

Prove **Theorem 4.5.4 (Product of Borel $\sigma$-Algebras)**: for Borel $\sigma$-algebras in Euclidean space,

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right).
$$

### Solution 7

Let $\pi_{1}:\mathbb R^{m+n}\to\mathbb R^{m}$ and $\pi_{2}:\mathbb R^{m+n}\to\mathbb R^{n}$ be the two coordinate projections. We first establish a helper fact: projections $\pi_{i}$ are continuous, so they pull Borel sets back to Borel sets.

**Helper fact**: for a continuous map $f:\mathbb R^{m+n}\to\mathbb R^{m}$ (here $f=\pi_{1}$), if $A\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$, then $f^{-1}\left(A\right)\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$.

Proof: the family $\left\{A\subseteq\mathbb R^{m}:f^{-1}\left(A\right)\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)\right\}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra (preimages preserve complements and countable unions, **Theorem 2.4.2 (Properties of Preimage Operations)**), and it contains all open sets ($f$ is continuous, so preimages of open sets are open and hence in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$); therefore it contains $\sigma\left(\text{open sets}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$. The helper fact holds.

**($\subset$) $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$.**

Take any measurable rectangle $A\times B$ ($A\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$, $B\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$). Then

$$
A\times B=\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(A\right)\cap\pi_{2}^{-1}\left(B\right).
$$

By the helper fact, $\pi_{1}^{-1}\left(A\right),\pi_{2}^{-1}\left(B\right)\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$, and their intersection is also in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$. Thus all measurable rectangles lie in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$; since $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing them, $\subseteq$ holds.

**($\supset$) $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$.**

By **Basic Knowledge: Open Sets as Countable Unions of Open Rectangles**, any open set $O$ in $\mathbb R^{m+n}$ is a countable union of open rectangles: $O=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\left(U_{k}\times V_{k}\right)$, where $U_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{m}$, $V_{k}\subset\mathbb R^{n}$ are open. Each open rectangle $U_{k}\times V_{k}$ is a measurable rectangle ($U_{k}\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)$, $V_{k}\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$), so it belongs to $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$. Hence $O$, as a countable union, also belongs to the product $\sigma$-algebra. Thus all open sets lie in $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$, and therefore

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)=\sigma\left(\text{open sets}\right)\subseteq\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right).
$$

Combining both directions gives $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right)$. $\blacksquare$

## 8

Let $\left\{\left(X_{\alpha},\mathcal F_{\alpha}\right)\right\}_{\alpha\in I}$ be a family of measurable spaces. Suppose $\mathcal F_{\alpha}=\sigma\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right)$, where $\mathcal E_{\alpha}$ is a family of subsets of $X_{\alpha}$. Prove that

$$
\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}=\sigma\left(\left\{\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right):\alpha\in I\right\}\right),
$$

where $\pi_{\beta}:\prod_{\alpha\in I}X_{\alpha}\to X_{\beta}$ is the coordinate projection from the product space onto $X_{\beta}$.

(Here $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$, by **Definition 4.5.7 (Arbitrary Product $\sigma$-Algebra)**, is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by all cylinder sets $\pi_{\beta}^{-1}\left(A_{\beta}\right)$ with $A_{\beta}\in\mathcal F_{\beta}$, $\beta\in I$.)

### Solution 8

Let $\mathscr G:=\sigma\left(\left\{\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right):\alpha\in I\right\}\right)$. Prove two inclusions.

**($\subset$) $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathscr G$.**

Fix $\alpha\in I$, consider $\pi_{\alpha}:\prod_{\beta\in I}X_{\beta}\to X_{\alpha}$, and define

$$
\mathscr C_{\alpha}:=\left\{A\subseteq X_{\alpha}:\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(A\right)\in\mathscr G\right\}.
$$

Since preimage operations preserve complements and countable unions (**Theorem 2.4.2 (Properties of Preimage Operations)**), $\mathscr C_{\alpha}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra. Moreover, $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right)\subseteq\mathscr G$ by the definition of $\mathscr G$, so $\mathcal E_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathscr C_{\alpha}$. Thus $\mathscr C_{\alpha}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal E_{\alpha}$, hence it contains $\sigma\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right)=\mathcal F_{\alpha}$. That is, for every $A\in\mathcal F_{\alpha}$, $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(A\right)\in\mathscr G$.

Since $\alpha\in I$ is arbitrary, all cylinder sets $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(A\right)$ ($A\in\mathcal F_{\alpha}$, $\alpha\in I$) lie in $\mathscr G$. Since $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$ is the $\sigma$-algebra generated by these cylinder sets,

$$
\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}\subseteq\mathscr G.
$$

**($\supset$) $\mathscr G\subseteq\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$.**

Fix $\alpha\in I$. For any $E_{\alpha}\in\mathcal E_{\alpha}$, $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(E_{\alpha}\right)$ is exactly a cylinder set (in **Definition 4.5.7 (Arbitrary Product $\sigma$-Algebra)** take $A_{\beta}=E_{\alpha}$ when $\beta=\alpha$), so $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right)\subseteq\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$. Thus all generators $\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(E_{\alpha}\right)$ ($\alpha\in I$) lie in the product $\sigma$-algebra, and since $\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}$ is a $\sigma$-algebra containing these sets, the $\sigma$-algebra they generate satisfies

$$
\mathscr G\subseteq\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}.
$$

Combining both directions,

$$
\bigotimes_{\alpha\in I}\mathcal F_{\alpha}=\sigma\left(\left\{\pi_{\alpha}^{-1}\left(\mathcal E_{\alpha}\right):\alpha\in I\right\}\right).
$$

$\blacksquare$
