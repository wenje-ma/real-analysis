## Algebras and Measurable Structures

**Definition 4.1.1** (Algebra of Sets)

Let $X$ be nonempty and $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. $\mathcal A$ is called an **algebra of sets** (Boolean algebra) on $X$ if it satisfies:

(1) $X\in\mathcal A$;

(2) closed under set difference: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\setminus B\in\mathcal A$;

(3) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

**Proposition 4.1.2** (Equivalent Definitions of an Algebra)

Let $\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. Then the following conditions are equivalent:

(1) $\mathcal A$ is an algebra;

(2) $\mathcal A$ satisfies:

(a) $X\in\mathcal A$;

(b) closed under complements: $A\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A^{c}=X\setminus A\in\mathcal A$;

(c) closed under finite unions: $A,B\in\mathcal A\Rightarrow A\cup B\in\mathcal A$.

**Definition 4.1.3** ($\sigma$-Algebra)

Let $X$ be nonempty and $\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. $\mathcal F$ is called a **$\sigma$-algebra** on $X$ if it satisfies:

(1) $X\in\mathcal F$;

(2) closed under complements: $A\in\mathcal F\Rightarrow A^{c}\in\mathcal F$;

(3) closed under countable unions:

$$
\left\{A_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F\Rightarrow\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal F.
$$

$\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ is called a **measurable space**, and the elements of $\mathcal F$ are called **measurable sets**.

**Definition 4.2.1** (Generated Algebra)

The smallest algebra containing $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is called the algebra generated by $\mathcal E$, denoted $\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)$:

$$
\alpha\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal A\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal A\text{ 是代数且 }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal A\right\}.
$$

**Definition 4.2.2** (Generated $\sigma$-Algebra)

The smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ is called the $\sigma$-algebra generated by $\mathcal E$, denoted $\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)$:

$$
\sigma\left(\mathcal E\right)=\bigcap\left\{\mathcal F\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right):\mathcal F\text{ 是 $\sigma$-代数且 }\mathcal E\subset\mathcal F\right\}.
$$

**Definition 4.3.2** (Borel $\sigma$-Algebra)

Let $\left(X,\tau\right)$ be a topological space. The $\sigma$-algebra generated by all open sets is called the **Borel $\sigma$-algebra**, denoted $\mathcal B\left(X\right)$; its elements are called **Borel sets**.

**Example 4.3.3** (The Borel Algebra on the Real Line)

On $\mathbb R$, the following families of sets all generate the same Borel $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)$: all open sets $\tau$, all closed sets $\mathcal F$, all open intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}$, all closed intervals $\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}$, all left-open right-closed intervals $\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}$, i.e.

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R\right)\\
&=\sigma\left(\tau\right)\\
&=\sigma\left(\mathcal F\right)\\
&=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right)\right\}\right)\\
&=\sigma\left(\left\{\left[a,b\right]\right\}\right)\\
&=\sigma\left(\left\{\left(a,b\right]\right\}\right).\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 4.4.1** (Monotone Class)

Let $\mathcal M\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$. $\mathcal M$ is called a **monotone class** if it satisfies:

(1) closed under monotonically increasing sequences: $A_{1}\subset A_{2}\subset\cdots$ and

$$
A_{n}\in\mathcal M\Rightarrow\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal M;
$$

(2) closed under monotonically decreasing sequences: $A_{1}\supset A_{2}\supset\cdots$ and

$$
A_{n}\in\mathcal M\Rightarrow\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}A_{n}\in\mathcal M.
$$

**Theorem 4.4.2** (Monotone Class Theorem)

Let $\mathcal A$ be an algebra and $\mathcal M$ a monotone class containing $\mathcal A$. Then $\sigma\left(\mathcal A\right)\subset\mathcal M$.

**Definition 4.5.1 / 4.5.2** (Measurable Rectangles and Product $\sigma$-Algebra)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal G\right)$ be measurable spaces. $A\times B\subseteq X\times Y$ is called a **measurable rectangle** if $A\in\mathcal F$ and $B\in\mathcal G$. The **product $\sigma$-algebra** is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing all measurable rectangles:

$$
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G:=\sigma\left(\left\{A\times B:A\in\mathcal F,B\in\mathcal G\right\}\right).
$$

**Definition 4.5.2** (Product $\sigma$-Algebra)

The product $\sigma$-algebra $\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$ is the smallest $\sigma$-algebra containing all measurable rectangles:

$$
\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G:=\sigma\left(\left\{A\times B:A\in\mathcal F,B\in\mathcal G\right\}\right).
$$

**Theorem 4.5.3** (Measurability of Sections)

If $E\in\mathcal F\otimes\mathcal G$, then for any $x\in X$ and $y\in Y$, its sections are measurable:

$$
\begin{aligned}E_{x}&=\left\{y\in Y:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal G,\\
E^{y}&=\left\{x\in X:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}\in\mathcal F.\end{aligned}
$$

**Theorem 4.5.4** (Products of Borel Sets)

For the Borel $\sigma$-algebras in Euclidean spaces, one has

$$
\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m}\right)\otimes\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)=\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{m+n}\right).
$$

## Measures and Measurable Sets

**Definition 5.1.2** (Measure)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ be a measurable space. A function $\mu:\mathcal F\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ is called a measure on $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ if it satisfies:

(1) nonnegativity: $\mu\left(E\right)\ge0$ for all $E\in\mathcal F$;

(2) null empty set: $\mu\left(\varnothing\right)=0$;

(3) countable additivity ($\sigma$-additivity): for any countably many pairwise disjoint $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$,

$$
\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Definition 5.1.5** ($\sigma$-Finite Measure)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ be a measure space. If there exists a sequence of measurable sets $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ such that $X=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}$ and $\mu\left(E_{n}\right)< +\infty$ for all $n\in\mathbb N$, then $\mu$ is called a $\sigma$-finite measure.

**Proposition 5.2.1** (Finite Additivity)

Let $\mu$ be a measure on $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ and $E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\in\mathcal F$ be pairwise disjoint sets. Then

$$
\mu\left(\bigcup_{k=1}^{n}E_{k}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mu\left(E_{k}\right).
$$

**Proposition 5.2.2** (Monotonicity)

Let $\mu$ be a measure, $E,F\in\mathcal F$ and $E\subset F$. Then $\mu\left(E\right)\le\mu\left(F\right)$. If in addition $\mu\left(E\right)< +\infty$, then

$$
\mu\left(F\setminus E\right)=\mu\left(F\right)-\mu\left(E\right).
$$

**Proposition 5.2.3** (Countable Subadditivity)

Let $\mu$ be a measure and $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ be any countable collection of sets (not necessarily pairwise disjoint). Then

$$
\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)\le\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Proposition 5.2.4** (Continuity from Below)

Let $\mu$ be a measure and $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ be a monotonically increasing sequence of sets (i.e. $E_{1}\subset E_{2}\subset\cdots$). Then

$$
\mu\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Proposition 5.2.5** (Continuity from Above)

Let $\mu$ be a measure and $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$ be a monotonically decreasing sequence of sets (i.e. $E_{1}\supset E_{2}\supset\cdots$), and suppose there exists some $m$ such that $\mu\left(E_{m}\right)< +\infty$. Then

$$
\mu\left(\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Definition 5.4.1** (Null Set)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ be a measure space. A set $N\in\mathcal F$ is called a null set if $\mu\left(N\right)=0$.

**Definition 5.4.2** (Set of Measure Zero)

A set $A\subset X$ is called a set of measure zero if there exists $N\in\mathcal F$ such that $A\subset N$ and $\mu\left(N\right)=0$.

**Definition 5.4.4** (Complete Measure)

A measure space $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ is called complete if for any $N\in\mathcal F$ with $\mu\left(N\right)=0$ and any $A\subset N$, one has $A\in\mathcal F$.

## Lebesgue Measure and Regularity

**Definition 6.1.1** (Outer Measure)

Let $X$ be nonempty. A function $\mu^{*}:\mathcal P\left(X\right)\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ is called an **outer measure** on $X$ if it satisfies:

(1) nonnegativity: $\mu^{*}\left(\varnothing\right)=0$, and $\mu^{*}\left(E\right)\ge0$ for all $E\subset X$;

(2) monotonicity:

$$
E\subset F\Rightarrow\mu^{*}\left(E\right)\le\mu^{*}\left(F\right);
$$

(3) countable subadditivity:

$$
\mu^{*}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)\le\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu^{*}\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Definition 6.2.1** (Premeasure)

Let $X$ be nonempty and $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ be an algebra. A function $\mu_{0}:\mathcal E\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ is called a **premeasure** if:

(1) $\mu_{0}\left(\varnothing\right)=0$;

(2) if $\left\{E_{n}\right\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal E$ are pairwise disjoint and

$$
\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\in\mathcal E
$$

, then

$$
\mu_{0}\left(\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{0}\left(E_{n}\right).
$$

**Theorem 6.2.2** (Outer Measure Generated by a Premeasure)

Let $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal P\left(X\right)$ be a covering class and $\mu_{0}:\mathcal E\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ a premeasure. For any $A\subset X$, define

$$
\mu^{*}\left(A\right)=\inf\left\{\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mu_{0}\left(E_{n}\right):E_{n}\in\mathcal E,A\subset\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}E_{n}\right\}.
$$

Then $\mu^{*}$ is an outer measure on $X$, and $\mu^{*}|_{\mathcal E}=\mu_{0}$.

**Definition 6.3.1** (Carathéodory Condition)

Let $\mu^{*}$ be an outer measure on $X$. A subset $A\subset X$ is called $\mu^{*}$-**measurable** if for any $T\subset X$,

$$
\mu^{*}\left(T\right)=\mu^{*}\left(T\cap A\right)+\mu^{*}\left(T\cap A^{c}\right).
$$

The collection of all $\mu^{*}$-measurable sets is denoted $\mathcal M_{\mu^{*}}$.

**Lemma 6.3.2**

If $\mathcal E$ is an algebra and $\mu_{0}$ is a premeasure, then $\mathcal E\subset\mathcal M_{\mu^{*}}$.

**Theorem 6.3.3** (Carathéodory's Theorem)

Let $\mu^{*}$ be an outer measure on $X$. Then:

(1) $\mathcal M$ is a $\sigma$-algebra;

(2) $\mu^{*}|_{\mathcal M}$ is a complete measure;

(3) if $\mu^{*}$ is generated by a premeasure $\mu_{0}$ and $\mu_{0}$ is $\sigma$-finite, then $\mu^{*}|_{\mathcal M}$ is the unique extension of $\mu_{0}$.

**Definition 6.4.1** (h-Intervals)

Let $-\infty\le a<  b<  \infty$. All left-open right-closed intervals $\left(a,b\right]$, as well as $\left(a,\infty\right)$ and $\varnothing$, are called **h-intervals**. The family $\mathcal E$ of finite disjoint unions of h-intervals forms an algebra.

**Proposition 6.4.2** (A Premeasure on $\mathcal E$)

Let $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be an increasing right-continuous function. For disjoint intervals $\left(a_{i},b_{i}\right]$, $i=1,\dots,n$, define

$$
\mu_{0}\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\left(a_{i},b_{i}\right]\right):=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left[F\left(b_{i}\right)-F\left(a_{i}\right)\right]
$$

, and set $\mu_{0}\left(\varnothing\right)=0$. Then $\mu_{0}$ is a premeasure on $\mathcal E$.

**Theorem 6.4.5** (Regularity of Lebesgue–Stieltjes Measures)

Let $F:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ be a right-continuous monotonically increasing function, $\mu_{F}$ the Lebesgue–Stieltjes measure generated by $F$, and $\mathcal M^{*}$ the $\sigma$-algebra of $\mu_{F}$-measurable sets. If $E\in\mathcal M^{*}$, then

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\mu_{F}\left(E\right)\\
&=\inf\left\{\mu_{F}\left(U\right):E\subset U,U\text{开}\right\}\\
&=\sup\left\{\mu_{F}\left(K\right):K\subset E,K\text{紧}\right\}.\end{aligned}
$$

**Definition 6.4.6** (Lebesgue Measure)

The measure generated by $F\left(x\right)=x$ is called the **Lebesgue measure**, denoted $m$; the measurable sets defined via the corresponding outer measure are called **Lebesgue measurable sets**, and their collection is denoted $\mathcal L$.

**Definition 6.5.1** (Recursive Construction of the Cantor Set)

Let $C_{0}=\left[0,1\right]$. For each $n\ge0$, define

$$
C_{n+1}=\frac{1}{3}C_{n}\cup\left(\frac{2}{3}+\frac{1}{3}C_{n}\right)
$$

. Then the **Cantor set** is defined as $C=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}$.

**Theorem 6.5.2** (Ternary Characterization of the Cantor Set)

A point $x\in\left[0,1\right]$ belongs to the Cantor set $C$ if and only if its ternary expansion

$$
x=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{k}}{3^{k}}
$$

contains no digit $1$ (i.e. $a_{k}\in\left\{0,2\right\}$).

## Measurable Functions

**Definition 7.1.1** (Measurable Map)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal G\right)$ be two measurable spaces. A map $f:X\to Y$ is called $\left(\mathcal F,\mathcal G\right)$-**measurable** if for any $B\in\mathcal G$, $f^{-1}\left(B\right)\in\mathcal F$.

**Proposition 7.1.5** (Composition Preserves Measurability)

If $f:X\to Y$ is $\left(\mathcal F,\mathcal G\right)$-measurable and $g:Y\to Z$ is $\left(\mathcal G,\mathcal H\right)$-measurable, then $g\circ f:X\to Z$ is $\left(\mathcal F,\mathcal H\right)$-measurable.

**Proposition 7.1.7** (Measurability on Product Spaces)

Let

$$
f=\left(f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\right):X\to Y_{1}\times\cdots\times Y_{n}
$$

. Under the product $\sigma$-algebra on the product space, $f$ is measurable if and only if each coordinate map $f_{i}:X\to Y_{i}$ is measurable.

**Theorem 7.1.8** (Closure of Real-Valued Measurable Functions Under Operations)

Let $f,g:X\to\mathbb R$ be measurable and $c\in\mathbb R$. Then:

(1) $cf$, $f+g$, $fg$, $\max\left(f,g\right)$, $\min\left(f,g\right)$ are measurable;

(2) if $g\left(x\right)\ne0$ for all $x$, then $f/g$ is measurable;

(3) if $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ is measurable, then $\sup_{n}f_{n}$, $\inf_{n}f_{n}$, $\limsup_{n\to\infty}f_{n}$, $\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}$ are measurable;

(4) if $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ converges pointwise, then the limit function $\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}$ is measurable.

**Theorem 7.1.9** (Composition of Measurable Functions with Continuous Functions)

Let $f:X\to\mathbb R$ be measurable and $g:\mathbb R\to\mathbb R$ continuous. Then $g\circ f:X\to\mathbb R$ is measurable.

**Theorem 7.2.2** (Construction of Measurable Functions)

If $f:X\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ is a nonnegative measurable function, then there exists a sequence of nonnegative simple functions $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ satisfying $0\le f_{1}\le f_{2}\le\cdots\le f$ and

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)
$$

for every $x\in X$.

**Example 7.3.5** (Indicator Function)

Let $A\subset X$. Then $\chi_{A}:X\to\mathbb R$ is measurable if and only if $A\in\mathcal F$.

## The Lebesgue Integral and Convergence Theorems

**Definition 8.1.1** (Integral of a Nonnegative Simple Function)

Let $f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\chi_{A_{i}}$ be a nonnegative simple function, where $c_{i}\ge0$, the $A_{i}\in\mathcal F$ are pairwise disjoint, and $\bigcup_{i}A_{i}=X$. The integral of $f$ with respect to $\mu$ is defined as

$$
\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mu\left(A_{i}\right)
$$

, with the convention $0\cdot\infty=0$.

**Proposition 8.1.2** (Linearity)

Let $f,g$ be nonnegative simple functions and $\alpha,\beta\ge0$. Then

$$
\int_{X}\left(\alpha f+\beta g\right)\mathrm{d}\mu=\alpha\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu+\beta\int_{X}g\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

**Definition 8.2.1** (Integral of a Nonnegative Measurable Function)

Let $f:X\to\left[0,+\infty\right]$ be a nonnegative measurable function. The integral of $f$ with respect to $\mu$ is defined as

$$
\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\sup\left\{\int_{X}\phi\,\mathrm{d}\mu:\phi\text{是简单函数},0\le\phi\le f\right\}
$$

. If $\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu< \infty$, then $f$ is called integrable.

**Theorem 8.2.2** (Monotone Convergence Theorem)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ be a measure space and $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ a sequence of nonnegative measurable functions satisfying $0\le f_{1}\left(x\right)\le f_{2}\left(x\right)\le\cdots$ for all $x\in X$, and $f_{n}\to f$ pointwise. Then

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

**Remark 8.2.3**

The monotone convergence theorem tells us that the integral can be approximated by simple functions.

**Proposition 8.2.4** (Absolute Continuity)

Let $f$ be a nonnegative measurable function. Then $A\mapsto\int_{A}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu$ is a measure; if $\mu\left(A\right)=0$, then $\int_{A}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0$.

**Definition 8.2.5** (Integral of a General Measurable Function)

Let $f:X\to\left[-\infty,+\infty\right]$ be a measurable function. The positive and negative parts of $f$ are defined by $f^{+}\left(x\right)=\max\left\{f\left(x\right),0\right\}$ and $f^{-}\left(x\right)=\max\left\{-f\left(x\right),0\right\}$. If at least one of the integrals $\int_{X}f^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu$, $\int_{X}f^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu$ is finite, then the integral of $f$ is defined as

$$
\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{X}f^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu-\int_{X}f^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu
$$

. If $\int_{X}f^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu< \infty$ and $\int_{X}f^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu< \infty$, then $f$ is called integrable.

**Theorem 8.2.7** (Fatou's Lemma)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ be a measure space and $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ a sequence of nonnegative measurable functions. Then

$$
\int_{X}\liminf_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu\le\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

**Theorem 8.2.9** (Dominated Convergence Theorem)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F,\mu\right)$ be a measure space, $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ a sequence of measurable functions, and $f_{n}\to f$ almost everywhere. If there exists an integrable function $g$ such that $\left|f_{n}\right|\le g$ almost everywhere for all $n$, then

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{X}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu=\int_{X}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

**Lemma 8.2.13** (Termwise Integration of Series)

Let $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ be a sequence of measurable functions and

$$
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{X}\left|f_{n}\right|\mathrm{d}\mu< \infty
$$

, then

$$
\int_{X}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{X}f_{n}\,\mathrm{d}\mu.
$$

**Theorem 8.2.16**

If $f\in L^{1}\left(\mu\right)$ and $\epsilon> 0$, then there exists an integrable simple function $\varphi=\sum a_{j}\chi_{E_{j}}$ such that

$$
\int\left|f-\varphi\right|\mathrm{d}\mu< \epsilon
$$

(i.e. integrable simple functions are dense in the $L^{1}$ metric).

**Theorem 8.2.17** (Parameter-Dependent Integrals)

Let $f:X\times\left[a,b\right]\to\mathbb R$, and suppose for each $t\in\left[a,b\right]$, the function $f\left(\cdot,t\right):X\to\mathbb R$ is integrable. Set

$$
F\left(t\right)=\int_{X}f\left(x,t\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right).
$$

(a) if there exists $g\in L^{1}\left(\mu\right)$ such that $\left|f\left(x,t\right)\right|\le g\left(x\right)$ for all $x,t$, and for each $x$,

$$
\lim_{t\to t_{0}}f\left(x,t\right)=f\left(x,t_{0}\right)
$$

, then

$$
\lim_{t\to t_{0}}F\left(t\right)=F\left(t_{0}\right);
$$

(b) if the partial derivative $\partial f/\partial t$ exists, and there exists $g\in L^{1}\left(\mu\right)$ such that for all $x,t$,

$$
\left|\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\left(x,t\right)\right|\le g\left(x\right)
$$

, then $F$ is differentiable, and

$$
F'\left(t\right)=\int\frac{\partial f}{\partial t}\left(x,t\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right).
$$

## Convergence of Sequences of Functions

**Definition 9.1.1** (Convergence Almost Everywhere)

Let $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ be a sequence of measurable functions and $f$ a measurable function. If there exists a set of measure zero $E$ such that for all $x\notin E$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}f_{n}\left(x\right)=f\left(x\right)
$$

, then $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ is said to converge to $f$ $\mu$-almost everywhere, denoted $f_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{a.e.}}f$.

**Proposition 9.1.2** (Characterization of a.e. Convergence)

$f_{n}\xrightarrow{\text{a.e.}}f$ if and only if for any $\epsilon> 0$,

$$
\mu\left(\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\bigcup_{n=k}^{\infty}\left\{x:\left|f_{n}\left(x\right)-f\left(x\right)\right|> \epsilon\right\}\right)=0.
$$

**Definition 9.1.3** (Convergence in Measure)

Let $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ be a sequence of measurable functions and $f$ a measurable function. If for any $\varepsilon> 0$,

$$
\lim_{n\to\infty}\mu\left(\left\{x:\left|f_{n}\left(x\right)-f\left(x\right)\right|> \varepsilon\right\}\right)=0
$$

, then $\left\{f_{n}\right\}$ is said to converge to $f$ in measure, denoted $f_{n}\xrightarrow{\mu}f$.

**Theorem 9.1.8** (Subsequence Property)

If $f_{n}\xrightarrow{\mu}f$, then there exists a subsequence $\left\{f_{n_{k}}\right\}$ such that $f_{n_{k}}\to f$ almost everywhere.

**Theorem 9.1.15** (Egorov's Theorem)

Let $\mu\left(X\right)< \infty$ and $f_{n}\to f$ almost everywhere. Then for any $\delta> 0$, there exists a measurable set $E\subset X$ such that $\mu\left(E\right)< \delta$, and on $X\setminus E$, $f_{n}$ converges uniformly to $f$. In particular, in a finite measure space, almost everywhere convergence implies convergence in measure.

## Product Measures and Iterated Integrals

**Definition 10.1.1** (Product $\sigma$-Algebra)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal A,\mu\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal B,\nu\right)$ be two measure spaces. The product $\sigma$-algebra on the product space $X\times Y$ is defined as

$$
\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B=\sigma\left(\left\{A\times B:A\in\mathcal A,B\in\mathcal B\right\}\right)
$$

, i.e. the $\sigma$-algebra generated by all measurable rectangles.

**Definition 10.1.2** (Sections)

For $E\subseteq X\times Y$, define the x-section of $E$ as $E_{x}=\left\{y\in Y:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}$ and the y-section as $E^{y}=\left\{x\in X:\left(x,y\right)\in E\right\}$.

**Theorem 10.1.4** (Existence and Uniqueness of Product Measures)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal A,\mu\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal B,\nu\right)$ be $\sigma$-finite measure spaces. There exists a unique measure $\mu\times\nu$ on $\left(X\times Y,\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B\right)$ such that for all $A\in\mathcal A$, $B\in\mathcal B$,

$$
\left(\mu\times\nu\right)\left(A\times B\right)=\mu\left(A\right)\nu\left(B\right)
$$

. It is constructed as

$$
\phi\left(E\right)=\int_{X}\nu\left(E_{x}\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right)
$$

for $E\in\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$.

**Theorem 10.2.1** (Fubini's Theorem)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal A,\mu\right)$ and $\left(Y,\mathcal B,\nu\right)$ be $\sigma$-finite measure spaces and $f:X\times Y\to\mathbb R$ an $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$-measurable function. If $f$ is integrable on $X\times Y$, i.e.

$$
\int_{X\times Y}\left|f\right|\mathrm{d}\left(\mu\times\nu\right)< \infty
$$

, then for almost every $x$, $y\mapsto f\left(x,y\right)$ is $\nu$-integrable; for almost every $y$, $x\mapsto f\left(x,y\right)$ is $\mu$-integrable; and the iterated integrals coincide:

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\int_{X\times Y}f\,\mathrm{d}\left(\mu\times\nu\right)\\
&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{Y}f\left(x,y\right)\mathrm{d}\nu\left(y\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right)\\
&=\int_{Y}\left(\int_{X}f\left(x,y\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\nu\left(y\right).\end{aligned}
$$

**Theorem 10.2.2** (Tonelli's Theorem)

If $f:X\times Y\to\left[0,\infty\right]$ is $\mathcal A\otimes\mathcal B$-measurable, then

$$
\begin{aligned}&\;\;\int_{X\times Y}f\,\mathrm{d}\left(\mu\times\nu\right)\\
&=\int_{X}\left(\int_{Y}f\left(x,y\right)\mathrm{d}\nu\left(y\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right)\\
&=\int_{Y}\left(\int_{X}f\left(x,y\right)\mathrm{d}\mu\left(x\right)\right)\mathrm{d}\nu\left(y\right),\end{aligned}
$$

 where all integral values lie in $\left[0,\infty\right]$.

## Signed Measures and the Radon–Nikodym Theorem

**Definition 11.1.1** (Signed Measure)

Let $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$ be a measurable space. A function $\nu:\mathcal F\to\mathbb R\cup\left\{+\infty\right\}$ or $\mathbb R\cup\left\{-\infty\right\}$ is called a **signed measure** if it satisfies:

(1) $\nu\left(\varnothing\right)=0$;

(2) countable additivity: for any sequence of pairwise disjoint measurable sets $\left\{E_{i}\right\}_{i=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal F$,

$$
\nu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}E_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\nu\left(E_{i}\right);
$$

(3) $\nu$ cannot take both $+\infty$ and $-\infty$ as values.

**Proposition 11.1.2** (Basic Properties of Signed Measures)

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure. Then:

(1) finite additivity: for any finitely many pairwise disjoint $E_{1},\dots,E_{n}\in\mathcal F$,

$$
\nu\left(\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\nu\left(E_{i}\right);
$$

(2) continuity from below: if $\left\{E_{j}\right\}$ is monotonically increasing and measurable, then

$$
\lim_{j\to\infty}\nu\left(E_{j}\right)=\nu\left(\bigcup_{j}E_{j}\right);
$$

(3) continuity from above: if $\left\{E_{j}\right\}$ is monotonically decreasing and measurable and $\nu\left(E_{1}\right)< \infty$, then

$$
\lim_{j\to\infty}\nu\left(E_{j}\right)=\nu\left(\bigcap_{j}E_{j}\right).
$$

**Definition 11.2.1** (Positive Set, Negative Set, Null Set)

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure on $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$.
A set $P\in\mathcal F$ is called a **positive set** (denoted $P\gtrsim0$) if for any $E\in\mathcal F$ with $E\subset P$, one has $\nu\left(E\right)\ge0$;
a set $N\in\mathcal F$ is called a **negative set** (denoted $N\lesssim0$) if for any $E\in\mathcal F$ with $E\subset N$, one has $\nu\left(E\right)\le0$;
a set $Z\in\mathcal F$ is called a **null set** if for any $E\in\mathcal F$ with $E\subset Z$, one has $\nu\left(E\right)=0$.

**Lemma 11.2.2** (Properties of Positive Sets)

(1) any measurable subset of a positive set is a positive set;

(2) the countable union of positive sets is a positive set;

(3) if $\left\{P_{n}\right\}$ is a sequence of positive sets, then $\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}P_{n}$ is a positive set.
The above properties also hold for negative sets (Remark 11.2.3).

**Lemma 11.2.4**

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure. If $E$ is not a negative set, then there exists a positive set $F\subset E$ such that $\nu\left(F\right)> 0$.

**Theorem 11.3.2** (Hahn Decomposition Theorem)

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure on $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$. Then there exists a decomposition $X=P\cup N$ of $X$, where $P$ is a positive set, $N$ is a negative set, and $P\cap N=\varnothing$; and this decomposition is unique up to a $\nu$-null set.

**Theorem 11.4.1**

There exists a unique Borel measure $\sigma=\sigma_{n-1}$ on $S^{n-1}$ such that $m^{*}=\rho\times\sigma$. If $f$ is a Borel measurable function on $\mathbb R^{n}$ and either $f\ge0$ or $f\in L^{1}\left(m\right)$, then

$$
\int_{\mathbb R^{n}}f\left(x\right)\mathrm{d}x=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{S^{n-1}}f\left(rx'\right)r^{n-1}\mathrm{d}\sigma\left(x'\right)\mathrm{d}r.
$$

**Theorem 11.4.3** (Jordan Decomposition Theorem)

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure on $\left(X,\mathcal F\right)$. Then there exists a unique pair of mutually singular positive measures $\nu^{+}$ and $\nu^{-}$ such that $\nu=\nu^{+}-\nu^{-}$.

**Definition 11.4.4** (Variation Measure)

Let $\nu$ be a signed measure with Jordan decomposition $\nu=\nu^{+}-\nu^{-}$.
$\nu^{+}$ is called the **positive variation** of $\nu$, $\nu^{-}$ the **negative variation** of $\nu$, and $\left|\nu\right|=\nu^{+}+\nu^{-}$ the **total variation measure** of $\nu$.

**Definition 11.5.1** (Absolute Continuity)

Let $\mu$ be a positive measure and $\nu$ a signed measure. $\nu$ is called absolutely continuous with respect to $\mu$ (denoted $\nu\ll\mu$) if for any $E\in\mathcal F$, $\mu\left(E\right)=0\Rightarrow\nu\left(E\right)=0$.

**Remark 11.5.3**

Let $f\in L^{1}\left(\mu\right)$. Then

$$
\lim_{\mu\left(E\right)\to0}\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0.
$$

**Theorem 11.5.5** (Radon–Nikodym Theorem)

Let $\mu$ be a $\sigma$-finite positive measure and $\nu$ a signed measure with $\nu\ll\mu$. Then there exists a measurable function $f:X\to\mathbb R$ such that

$$
\nu\left(E\right)=\int_{E}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu
$$

holds for all $E\in\mathcal F$; and

$$
\nu^{+}\left(E\right)=\int_{E}f^{+}\mathrm{d}\mu,
$$

$$
\nu^{-}\left(E\right)=\int_{E}f^{-}\mathrm{d}\mu,
$$

$$
\left|\nu\right|\left(E\right)=\int_{E}\left|f\right|\mathrm{d}\mu
$$

. The function $f$ is called the Radon–Nikodym derivative of $\nu$ with respect to $\mu$, denoted $f=\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu}{\mathrm{d}\mu}$.

**Property 11.5.6** (Linearity)

If $\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\ll\mu$ and $a,b\in\mathbb R$, then

$$
\frac{\mathrm{d}\left(a\nu_{1}+b\nu_{2}\right)}{\mathrm{d}\mu}=a\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{1}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}+b\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu_{2}}{\mathrm{d}\mu}
$$

holds $\mu$-almost everywhere.

## Bounded Variation and Absolute Continuity

**Corollary 13.4.5**

Let $F$ be a right-continuous increasing function on $\mathbb R$. Then $F'\left(x\right)$ exists for almost every $x$.

**Definition 14.0.6** (Functions of Bounded Variation)

Let $f:\left[a,b\right]\to\mathbb R$. For any partition $P:a=x_{0}< x_{1}< \cdots< x_{n}=b$ of $\left[a,b\right]$, define the variation

$$
V\left(f,P\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left|f\left(x_{i}\right)-f\left(x_{i-1}\right)\right|
$$

. The total variation of $f$ on $\left[a,b\right]$ is

$$
V_{a}^{b}\left(f\right)=\sup_{P}V\left(f,P\right)
$$

. If $V_{a}^{b}\left(f\right)< +\infty$, then $f$ is called a function of bounded variation on $\left[a,b\right]$, denoted $f\in BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$.

**Theorem 14.0.7** (Basic Properties)

Let $f,g\in BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$. Then:

(1) $BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$ forms a linear space;

(2) $f$ is a bounded function;

(3) if $f$ satisfies a Lipschitz condition, then $f\in BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$;

(4) every monotone function is a function of bounded variation.

**Theorem 14.0.10** (Discontinuity-Point Properties)

If $f\in BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$, then:

(1) $f$ has at most countably many discontinuity points;

(2) all discontinuity points are jump discontinuities of the first kind.

**Definition 15.0.11** (Absolute Continuity)

A function $f:\left[a,b\right]\to\mathbb R$ is called **absolutely continuous** (written $f\in AC\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$) if for any $\varepsilon> 0$, there exists $\delta> 0$ such that for any finite collection of pairwise disjoint subintervals $\left(a_{i},b_{i}\right)\subset\left[a,b\right]$, whenever $\sum_{i}\left(b_{i}-a_{i}\right)< \delta$, one has

$$
\sum_{i}\left|f\left(b_{i}\right)-f\left(a_{i}\right)\right|< \varepsilon.
$$

**Theorem 15.0.14** (Equivalent Characterization)

$f\in AC\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$ if and only if there exists $g\in L^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)$ such that

$$
f\left(x\right)=f\left(a\right)+\int_{a}^{x}g\left(t\right)\mathrm{d}t
$$

for all $x\in\left[a,b\right]$. In this case, $f'\left(x\right)=g\left(x\right)$ almost everywhere.

**Theorem 15.0.16**

The following strict inclusions hold: $C^{1}\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)\subset$ Lipschitz

$$
\subset AC\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)\subset BV\left(\left[a,b\right]\right)\subset
$$

bounded functions.

## Basic Knowledge

**Basic Knowledge**
**The Riemann Integral and Darboux Sums**: Let $f$ be a bounded real-valued function on $\left[a,b\right]$. For a partition $P=\left\{t_{0}< \cdots<  t_{n}\right\}$ of $\left[a,b\right]$, set

$$
M_{i}=\sup_{\left[t_{i-1},t_{i}\right]}f,
$$

$$
m_{i}=\inf_{\left[t_{i-1},t_{i}\right]}f
$$

, define the upper Darboux sum

$$
\overline S\left(P\right)=\sum_{i}M_{i}\left(t_{i}-t_{i-1}\right)
$$

, the lower Darboux sum

$$
\underline S\left(P\right)=\sum_{i}m_{i}\left(t_{i}-t_{i-1}\right)
$$

. The upper integral

$$
\overline{\int}f=\inf_{P}\overline S\left(P\right)
$$

, the lower integral

$$
\underline{\int}f=\sup_{P}\underline S\left(P\right)
$$

. If $\overline{\int}f=\underline{\int}f$, their common value is called the Riemann integral of $f$, and in that case $f$ is called Riemann integrable.

**Basic Knowledge** (Hardy–Littlewood Maximal Function)

Let $f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$. The Hardy–Littlewood maximal function of $f$ is defined by

$$
Mf\left(x\right)=\sup_{r> 0}\frac{1}{\left|B\left(x,r\right)\right|}\int_{B\left(x,r\right)}\left|f\left(y\right)\right|\mathrm{d}y,
$$

where $B\left(x,r\right)$ is the open ball centered at $x$ with radius $r$, and $\left|B\left(x,r\right)\right|$ denotes its Lebesgue measure.

**Basic Knowledge** (Ball-Volume Formula)

Let $C_{n}=m\left(B\left(1,0\right)\right)$ be the Lebesgue measure of the unit ball. Then for any $x\in\mathbb R^{n}$, $r> 0$,

$$
m\left(B\left(x,r\right)\right)=C_{n}r^{n}.
$$

**Basic Knowledge** (Regular Borel Measure)

Let $\nu$ be a Borel measure on $\mathbb R^{n}$. $\nu$ is called **regular** if:

(1) for every compact set $K$, $\nu\left(K\right)< \infty$;

(2) for every $E\in\mathcal B\left(\mathbb R^{n}\right)$,

$$
\nu\left(E\right)=\inf\left\{\nu\left(U\right):U\text{开集},E\subset U\right\}.
$$

**Basic Knowledge** (Normalized Bounded Variation $NBV$)

$$
NBV=\left\{F\in BV:F\text{右连续},F\left(-\infty\right)=0\right\}
$$

, where $BV$ denotes the functions of bounded variation on $\mathbb R$, and $T_{F}$ denotes the total variation function of $F$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Series)

The geometric series $\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}2^{-n}=1$ converges; the harmonic series $\sum_{k}\frac{1}{k}$ diverges.

**Basic Knowledge** (Normalized Bounded Variation $NBV$ and Stieltjes Measures)

$$
NBV=\left\{F\in BV:F\ \text{右连续},\ F\left(-\infty\right)=0\right\}
$$

. For an increasing right-continuous $F$, the formula

$$
\mu_{F}\left(\left(a,b\right]\right)=F\left(b\right)-F\left(a\right)
$$

uniquely determines a regular Borel measure $\mu_{F}$.

**Basic Knowledge** (Total Variation of an Absolutely Continuous Function)

If $F$ is absolutely continuous on $\left[a,b\right]$, then the total variation of $F$

$$
V_{a}^{b}\left(F\right)=\int_{a}^{b}\left|F'\left(t\right)\right|\mathrm{d}t.
$$
